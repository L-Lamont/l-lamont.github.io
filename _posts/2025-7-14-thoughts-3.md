## Competition

2 things have begun to scare me regret and not being ambitious enough. I don't 
have many regrets but the one that I tend to dwell on is sport. I played at a 
decent level but just stopped and never went back to the high level 
competition. Reflecting on my life now I don't actively try and compete in 
anything, even though competing is where I derived most of my meaning and 
purpose from earlier in life. As a kid being good at things was important 
and competition gave me a marker for comparison.

Some of the lack of competition is because comparison to peers use to be very 
simple and the games have changed or we stopped playing. Who won the game, who 
scored higher in the test, but now as we transition to take on more 
responsibities in life how do you compare anything? It all seems arbitrary to 
an extent. So now the options are, navigate the transition in what you derive 
meaning from, find different things to compete in, go back to what you use to 
compete in, find ways to compete with yourself.

Perhaps it is not competition that I'm missing it's structure. Competitions are 
usually structured but structures are not necessarily competitions. For 
instance when not working remotely I find it far easier to work and do better 
work as there is some level of structure and accountability imposed on me. But 
what about for things outside of work how do you decide what structure to set 
up (I guess you just start experimenting).

I am still figuring out what is happening in this post, maybe I will add to it 
maybe I will write in another post. But some key takeaways for me for now are

- I use to derive a lot of meaning from competitions and from structure and 
  probably still would I just don't compete anymore
- Competeting with yourself for things that are not easy to measure is probably 
  the easiest way to go
- Don't stop competing with others though as generally it will be structured and 
  regular interactions and competition is good for you and pushes you further
