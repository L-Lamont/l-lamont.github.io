## An Incomprehensive List of Books I liked

### Musashi related books
If you read my post "thoughts 0" (don't worry no one else read it either). You 
would of heard my admiration for the book written by Eiji Yoshikawa and the 
portrayal of the character. To me it is a narrative of someone trying to 
improve & get better at living his life. Whether that was his art, his 
swordsmanship or his understanding of zen.

I have also read and enjoyed the book of five rings, a book that Musashi 
actually wrote himself. It has some very simple lessons which are worth 
remembering and thinking about. After reading Musashi the novel it was 
interesting to read something that came directly from the source (or well at 
least through less interpretation).

Following on from the book of five rings I actually reread Musashi but now I am 
just finishing "The Lone Samurai" by William Scott Wilson. It was definitely 
more detailed & attempted to only show the fact. This is a good read for 
people already interested in Musashi but I would caution you to not to read 
everything word by word, I had to skim large portions of the book where I was 
not interested in the details. For example a discussion of the details of his 
art was not interesting to me but the discussion of influences on the book of 
five rings was fascinating. The author is far more knowledgable about how 
Musashi's work and work depicting him fits into Japanese culture & the world at 
large which as a novice is intriguing but at times overwhelming.


### Shogun
One my most enjoyable reads, after Musashi I was fascinated by Samurai and 
Japan during that time period. I was simply unable to put this book down whilst 
reading it over the course a month (along side a few other books). 


### Shoe Dog
This was a great book for me, it got me into running even though it is barely 
about it. It gives a great perspective on the challenges of starting a business 
and how people will try and diminish and squash you at every point. Luckily for 
the business in question no one succeeded and we now all know the name NIKE. 
This book came to me at a funny time, I had known about this book for a long 
time (it was released in 2016) and I didn't end up reading it until 2025. The 
book starts the story when the author was 24, just graduating college & setting 
off to travel around the world. Nothing crazy, but I finally got around to 
reading this book when I was 24, just finished university & wondering what to 
do with my life. I know it was just a coincidence but this was really helpful 
for my understanding everything is going to be fine and I will figure it out.


### Brandon Sanderson Books
I can pretty confidently say if you are looking for fantasy you should check 
any of Brandon Sanderson's books out. Not only are they just great books there 
are so many of them and he is a prolific writer so more a coming every year. I
started with Mistborn and am now also up to date with the Stormlight Archive. I 
don't know where I would reccommend starting but I really enjoyed the Wax & 
Wayne books in mistborn, but that only starts in the 4th book of the Mistborn 
series. The best storyline in the Stormlight Archive to me is Kaladin which you 
can get from book 1 so maybe start there... I don't know they are both great 
series. Also there are some good standalone books set in the same universe (or 
cosmere as it is known) which are really good and a bit shorter than some of 
other books.


### Books I will reread or relisten to
- Poor Charlie's Almanack
  - I listened to this as an audiobook and got immense value from it but I feel 
    it warrants a closer reading or at least a closer studying
- Musashi
  - I don't know why I want to read this again but I just really like it
- The Book of Five Rings
  - There are a lot of little things that I missed that was brought up in The 
    Lone Samurai which make me want to go back & see if I can put some more 
    things together

### Books I haven't got into
- The Gulag Archipelago
  - Was just a tough long read which eventually got the better of me
  - I found the subject matter very heavy and the writing dense
- Blood Meridian
  - Cormac McCarthy writes unlike anyone else I have read and cuts to the core 
    of what the story is
  - Sadly I find this writing style amazing to listen to but very hard to read, 
    so I have put it down with some tentative hopes to pick it up again soonish
